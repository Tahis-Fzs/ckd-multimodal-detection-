4.3.9
Clinical Decision-Support Prototype Demonstration
A Streamlit prototype was used to assess the locked MIMIC branch, as a research demonstration. The
calibrated admission risk, the threshold customization and the review of example-cases are part of the
interface, as well as the hand entry of features, and the global SHAP summary tab based on a frozen
test-set analysis. The NHANES, WESAD and live fusion combination is not integrated since it was
obvious that it wasn't within the model's range of evidence and scope in the prototype.
This demonstration shows that offline model outputs can be presented in an inspectable decision-
support workflow with explicit disclaimers and no therapeutic recommendations. It is not clinical
deployment, live EHR integration, or prospective validation [39], [42].
4.3.10
Discussion
However, the findings, when combined, suggest three conclusions in terms of this study.
Firstly, sigmoid-calibrated logistic regression, on the primary branch of admission to MIMIC,
achieved a sensible trade-off between discrimination and reliability. It attained AUROC 0.761 with
ECE 0.020, while similar results were obtained with logistic regression with AUROC 0.761 and ECE
0.249 (no calibration), and AUROC ~0.768 with ECE ~0.20 with the tree-based models. So,
calibrated probabilities should be used instead of only ranking performance to support decisions.
Second, the NHANES branch demonstrated very high population discrimination (0.992) which should
not be assumed as hospital admission discrimination. There are differences in setting, in definition of
label, in features available and their rates of use, and results from the branches should not be averaged
or merely applied to the inpatient setting.
Third, the wearable and fusion components remain methodological rather than clinically validated
CKD detectors. WESAD was evaluated using a stress-versus-baseline proxy because CKD labels
were unavailable in that cohort, while NHANES and MIMIC-IV used branch-specific CKD-related
labels under different settings. Late fusion across disjoint public cohorts also did not show patient-
