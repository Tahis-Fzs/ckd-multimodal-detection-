In the end, the chosen approach has provided the necessary rigorous train–validation–test split, which
has split the subjects or participants in groups based on their levels. Random splitting was considered,
since it would result in all the optimistic, clinically unrealistic estimates of performance given by
having multiple records of the same person on multiple partitions [27]. In particular, Sigmoid
calibration was favoured over isotonic calibration, since the isotonic method is more flexible but has a
greater tendency to over-fit on smaller validation sets, and has a more simpler calibration curve [14].
In this study, class balancing (class_weight) and the ensemble was also tested and used to prevent the
naive unweighted training of the models, although the proportion of CKD was low in NHANES and
MIMIC, a naive unweighted training would bias the model towards the majority negative class,
decreasing the sensitivity for screening use [47]. On top of that, it was suggested to do the extensive
experimental auto search but this was not done due to the time and the computation involved.
Reproducibility was maintained through fixed, documented experimental configurations rather than
extensive automated hyperparameter search. The upside of this approach is less optimism and no
reporting artifacts and reality based probability of threshold policies; the down side of this approach is
some loss of performance due to the limited search. Easy on the calculations; can be repeated using
the same workstation. Finally, the frozen calibrated model and the objects to be pre-processed are
loaded directly for inference to ensure the same results for the entire notebook pipeline and Streamlit
prototype.
3.3
Project Plan
There were 5 stages of the project from proposal to final submission. As it became clear what these
datasets were possible to access, what did not fit well in the modelling and what was possible to
complete with disjoint-cohort multimodal fusion, the plan was tweaked accordingly.
Phase 1 Research and planning, (Week 12 – 20) includes literature review, gap analysis, requirements
specification. CKD staging, EHR-based risk modelling, and wearable health monitoring were
reviewed [1]–[5], [16]–[18], [23], [33], [34]. The system architecture, data-flow design, as well as the
use of predefined public datasets with protocol level late fusion was defined.
Weeks 18-26: Phase 2, Data engineering, NHANES, MIMIC-IV, and WESAD data was acquired and
preprocessed. CKD labels, leakage-safe properties, splits groups and branch specific feature pipelines
were set up. To obtain more reliable imputation of train-only and scaling were applied before
validation and testing.
During Phase 3 (Weeks 24-36), logistic regression, RF, XGBoost, MLP, and ResMLP models were
trained and compared based on the branch models. The hyperparameters and sigmoid calibration were
done using validation data. The same model MIMIC was locked, as calibrated logistic regression,
with frozen checkpoints for reporting and prototype use.
