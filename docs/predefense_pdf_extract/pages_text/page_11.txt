Chapter 1
Introduction
The chapter sets out the motivation and aims of the project and outlines its methodology and expected
outcomes as well as formulating the overall structure of the project. It offers a glimpse into the early
Chronic Kidney Disease risk prediction problem and introduces a proposed explainable multi-modal
decision-support framework.
1.1
Introduction
CKD is a slowly progressive disease where kidney function decreases over a period of time as shown
by a declining eGFR or kidney damage in terms of structure or function [2], [18]. The severity of the
disease varies and can lead to end stage renal failure (ESRF), dialysis and transplant which is very
costly [2]. The prevalence of CKD is found worldwide, it often accompanies hypertension and type 2
diabetes and it is a condition that may not present with symptoms in the initial stages and therefore
not be diagnosed [2], [18]. Evidence of decreased kidney function or albuminuria early makes it
possible to intervene with meds and a modification of the diet and management of risk factors [17],
[18]. It is therefore a challenge for the nephrology and the preventive medicine community to be able
to identify high-risk persons so early.
Serum creatinine, eGFR using the CKD-EPI equation and urine ACR [17] have been the laboratory
markers traditionally used for the diagnosis of CKD. They are clinically useful, but are not continuous,
do not reflect the renal function, and are cross-sectional and dependent on time of day, muscle mass
and hydration, and also on the presence of other illnesses [1]. Laboratory screening is also hamstrung
by its intermittent lag time between clinic visits [2] and imaging has proven to be costly and less
available with the added problem of the inability to continuously monitor patients [2]. Thus, novel
diagnostic methods are needed that can detect any evolving alterations in the physiologic state before
renal dysfunction becomes evident, in this high-risk group.
There are new opportunities for using Artificial Intelligence (AI) in clinical risk prediction. Structured
and high dimensional patient data can be leveraged by analysing such data with machine learning or
deep learning algorithms, thus discovering complex nonlinear relationships which can supersede a
humanly-designed clinical scoring system in many cases [1], [13], [43]. Using deep learning on EHR
data has enabled better prediction of outcomes using temporal modelling on EHR data [8]. However,
the existing approaches to predict a CKD are mostly performed at present on one particular modality
of information, and fail to integrate complementary information from the other modality such as
wearable physiological sensors [1], [13], [32], [35]. Multi-modal wearable signals and clinical records
in structured format in the context of CKD remains an area that is yet to be explored in modelling [36].
However, another restriction is its interpretability. Clinicians in a critical clinical context would have
to view and understand the contributions of the inputs to the prediction, even in the event of good
discriminative performance of a model [46], [49], [50]. In the area of CKD prediction, understanding
feature attributions with explainable AI (XAI) methods and calibrated probabilistic outputs, and
framing them as decision support, are not sufficiently developed and remain as non-reproducible
elements of the same pipeline.
The next major challenge addressed in the research is the need for a multi-modal framework which
uses both EHR
and clinical data along with wearable, physiological signals, is able to obtain
estimates of personalized and calibrated risk scores, is explainable, and is feasible given lack of
patient-level alignment with public EHR, clinical, and non-clinical datasets. This thesis tries to solve
this issue by proposing a multi-modal deep learning framework, by connecting these data sources in a
probability level late fusion [36] and explainable with SHAP-based explainability [15] in order to
support transparent clinical decision making, instead of the autonomy of diagnosis [1], [42], [49].
