4.3
Results and Discussion
4.3.1
Individual Model Performance
Performance of the main classifiers in the hospital on the held out MIMIC test set of 5,997 admissions,
summarized in table 4.3. There was no significant differences between model families as
discrimination was fairly consistent AUROC 0.761 to 0.768. The highest AUROC 0.768 was obtained
for the XGBoost model, followed closely by random Forest model 0.768 and Tabular MLP model
0.766. Random Forest achieved the best accuracy 0.684 and F1 score 0.423 among the models, that
have not been calibrated. In fact, logistic regression achieved a similar performance in ranking
AUROC 0.761 but significantly higher calibration error ECE 0.249, whereas Tabular MLP exhibited
competitive ranking AUROC 0.766 as well as a relatively low calibration error ECE 0.028.
The results here suggest that while raising the capacity of the models did not lead to a clear
discrimination in favour of the candidates, the certainty of the probability of being predicted was an
important factor in candidating more than just the ranking by itself [14], [47].

4.3.2
Evaluation of the Locked Primary Model
Model selection prioritized sigmoid-calibrated logistic regression over uncalibrated baselines and tree
ensembles because it offered similar discrimination (AUROC 0.761) with substantially better
probability reliability (ECE 0.020).
On the test set, using a threshold of 0.16, the model achieved an accuracy of 0.692, F1-score of 0.415,
sensitivity of 0.674, specificity of 0.696, AUROC of 0.761, and AUPRC of 0.379. Moreover, the
scores on the calibration were quite satisfactory as can be seen from the score of ECE of 0.020 and the
score of Brier of 0.119.
The AUROC value for the uncalibrated logistic regression model with the cutoff of 0.52 was similar
to this proposed model 0.761, however the uncalibrated logistic regression model showed
substantially worse calibration (ECE = 0.249) than the sigmoid-calibrated primary model (ECE =
0.020), while both retained similar AUROC (0.761) and also the F1-score for the proposed model was
nearly equal to the uncalibrated logistic regression model which was 0.417 vs. 0.422. On the other
hand, isotonic calibration further decreased the ECE = 0.014 but with a compromise on the values of
accuracy = 0.630 and F1 = 0.404 at a threshold value of 0.12.
Overall, the calibrated model, which is of sigmoid form would be the best compromise to be used for
decision support applications. We found it to be clinically interpretable with linear structure, with
stable ranking performance, as well as being well calibrated probabilities, a feature that has been
typically used for threshold based risk communication [42], [49].
