Explainability limitations: SHAP attributions do not include the mechanics of any association,
just only statistical. There can be coding and socioeconomic bias in the explanations, which rely
on the manner in which information is documented in the sending hospital. The fitted linear
model refers to the local distribution of data from clinical settings, and local explanations are
only valid for this case.
6.3
Future Work
Future work should be continued down realistic lines existing and based on evidence; with expansion
of the current implementation.

Aligned multimodal cohorts: Collect or synthesize datasets in which the clinical records and
wearable sensors are the same patients, with the sole idea being that of exploring the usefulness
of early fusion of data and multimodal benefit at the patient level.

Prospective and external clinical validation: Test the hospital (admission) model on external
databases, future workflows, as well as on separate hospitals, while at the same time get familiar
with the temporal checking able within MIMIC.

Richer hospital modalities: Add other physiological and time series information other than the
many tabular data at the admission level like the waveform data available in the extended
versions of MIMIC.

Fairness and bias mitigation: Do auditing from the various the sub-populations with varying
demographic features, insurance coverage and documentation and investigate fairness-aware
training and threshold-based policies.

Clinical integration: Set up interoperability and workflow integration with standards-based
approach with nephrology and hospital stakeholders and try out clinician in the loop.

Operational deployment research: Avoid conducting any pilot outside of the research context
not featuring the calibration drift monitoring, model governance and versioning.
These directions taken together may take the framework from a research prototype into one that is
responsibly designed and calibrated as a decision-support tool.
