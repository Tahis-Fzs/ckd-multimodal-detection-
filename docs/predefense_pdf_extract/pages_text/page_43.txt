4.3.5
NHANES Population Branch Results
The test-set for the NHANES is an independent study population and their test-set results are
presented in the NHANES Table 4.6. XGBoost achieved the strongest overall results, accuracy 0.966,
F1 0.798, AUROC 0.992, ECE 0.014. Logistic regression without change of AUROC 0.982 and F1
0.692 was very discriminative and there was no significant change in sensitivity and specificity which
were 0.928 and 0.940. The calibration error ECE 0.053 was the only one that was small. ResMLP had
the best accuracy score of all classifiers which was 0.962, had the highest specificity 0.984 and the
lowest sensitivity 0.681 among all the classifiers when considering with logistic regression.
In summary, the results indicate that four features are built-in to NHANES population and lab, but
they can be appropriately used independently and in combination to successfully separate NHANES
CKD-risk into varying levels. Limiting this branch to reflect hospital admission or the average of
MIMIC metrics should be avoided since the cohorts are different, as are the setting, label construction
and features available [16], [17].

4.3.6
Wearable Proxy Branch Results
A wearable Random Forest classifier with stress versus base line proxy task was tested, with 578
windows, 15 subjects and was found to have AUROC 0.764, F1 0.667, Accuracy 0.812, Sensitivity
0.528 and Specificity 0.969 with ECE of 0.123. This was low sensitivity, high specificity which is
typically observed in stress-vs-baseline proxy task.
This branch was included to evaluate the processing and classification capability of this type of
Window in a reproducible manner in the multimodal approach. Not only, it's not just the one that does
not classify the patient as CKD, it's not the measure of kidney outcome. It has not been tested on
branch level yet and in the bigger study can only be measured as a methodological test and provide
probabilities if tested in the branch level using a late fusion protocol.
4.3.7
Multimodal Fusion Protocol Evaluation
Branch-level late fusion was not reported as a primary outcome because NHANES, MIMIC-IV, and
WESAD are disjoint public cohorts without shared patient identifiers. Under proxy conditions, fusion
performance is not interpretable as multimodal CKD detection.
