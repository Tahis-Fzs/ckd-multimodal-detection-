Overall, the project is best thought of as a research framework for branch-level leakage-aware,
calibrated and explainable hospital admission risk-stratification, in view of branch-level evidence,
SHAP review, and a Streamlit decision-support prototype. Before deployment to the real world, there
would be need for prospective clinical validation as well as same patient multimodal data.
4.4
Summary
A testing and evaluation infrastructure was introduced as a stepping stone to the implementation of
the proposed multimodal CKD risk prediction framework and empirical results as part of the proposed
framework was introduced in this chapter. This software stack, defensive design grouping and
discrimination metrics, model calibration metrics and a phased testing process were also experimented
with prior to reporting. Last, and certainly not least, a model, the sigmoid calibrated logistic
regression, is a locked model that might have been applied in the main MIMIC cohort of admissions
and these models are able to provide just as good, and indeed far better, baselines with much better
probability reliability level. A more intensive patient-level validations of the multimodal approach
were required, however, as well as limited usage of the proxy which is worn by patients with the
WESAD system. Using SHAP analysis and Streamlit prototype, the explanation of the decision
support was presented and visualised. The chapter emphasizes and illustrates and explains the case of:
risk stratification evidence not necessarily used in patients, but based on the fact that a set of markers
is present in the public data sets.
