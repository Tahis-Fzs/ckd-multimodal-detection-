Chapter 3
Research Methodology
This chapter presents the research methodology adopted for the proposed CKD risk prediction
framework, describing the datasets, preprocessing, feature extraction, model development, multimodal
fusion, explainability, and evaluation design. It explains how each methodological stage connects to
the overall goal of building an explainable, multimodal, decision-support system for early CKD risk
prediction.
3.1
Methodology
A structured research methodology was performed to develop the proposed CKD risk prediction
framework, which clearly aids the research purpose. The methodology goes hand-in-hand with a
multi-branch pipeline. Three types of data, population clinical, hospital EHR data and wearable
physiological data, are all independently modelled and processed, and their respective predictions
directly combined at the probability level. Goal is to get every stage of this pipeline clearly presented
and reproducible from data acquisition through to preprocessing, feature extraction, model
development, fusion, explainability and evaluation. One stage is then described in detail in each of the
subsections. As far as possible, the same methodology is adhered to throughout with the code adopted
and the experimental set-up locked, to represent the system as constructed, and to meet the
requirement of an explainable multimodal decision support tool for early prediction of CKD.
3.1.1
Overview
The research activity proposed is a stride towards the effort of early detection of patients who are
prone to developing Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD) which has no symptom in the initial stage of the
disease. For this purpose, the chapter builds upon three different, yet complementary health data types
and processes them through an explainable and reproducible workflow to a prototype of an
explainable decision support tool.
Three kinds of data were utilized: Population clinical data (NHANES); hospital EHR data (MIMIC-
IV); and portable wearable, physiological data (WESAD). These data-set are classified as individual
branches and are preprocessed, feature preparation and predictive-models are found for each one of
these data-sets. In each of the branches, machine learning and deep learning models were developed
and compared with one another and discrimination and probabilistic calibration was performed, which
might lead to the selection of models capable of giving reliable risk estimates.
Finally all the branch outputs are fused via a performance weighted method, that is a calibrated risk
probability of each branch is fused. To achieve the transparency, a complementary explainability
modular component is added to the main model based on SHAP and it generates feature level
explanations, implemented as a prototype decision support system instead of diagnostic tool [15].
